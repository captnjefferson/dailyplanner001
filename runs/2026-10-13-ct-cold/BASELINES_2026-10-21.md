# Baseline emails, by the ask (2026-10-21)

**What this is.** The baseline email for each of the four asks: the shared spine every personal draft in that group is built on. Each one is drawn from the reviewed drafts already in the tracker. Per person, only the bracketed parts change: the first line, which quotes something they said or ran, and sometimes the two questions. Every baseline follows the same rules: one person, no cc, no pitch, no ask for a call, no links, under 140 words. The subject follows your 9/22 sends ("Question about Brookside's education programs"). Nothing is sent from here.

## 1. Speak to their peer group (Entrepreneurs' Organization learning chairs and Vistage chairs)

**Subject:** Question about [chapter or group]'s speaker calendar

Hi [first name] –

[One thing from their own record, quoted exactly: a line from a podcast or post, or what's on their chapter's calendar this fall.]

Last spring I took six small businesses through AI implementation, and I'm trying to work out what's still unsolved in CEO rooms. Two questions, since you're the one building the calendar:

When AI comes up among your members, what's the worry under it – keeping up, or what it does to their people?

And when one member gets AI working, does it spread to the others, or stay inside that one company?

Glad to send back what other chairs say.

-Jefferson

## 2. Speak at their firm's owner event (hosts at banks, accounting, law and insurance firms)

**Subject:** Question about [event name]

Hi [first name] –

[One thing from their room: a past session, a line from its takeaway, or a question someone asked from the floor.]

Last spring I took six small businesses through AI implementation, and I'm trying to work out what's still unsolved for owners. Two questions about [event name]:

When AI comes up with the owners in your room, is it about growth – getting to the next stage – or the worry about their people?

And when you ask them what they want to hear next, has AI come up yet?

Glad to send you what other hosts tell me.

-Jefferson

## 3. Advise their company (founders of 25-to-100-person companies)

**Subject:** Question about [company]

Hi [first name] –

[One thing they said themselves about AI or running the company, quoted exactly, with where and when.]

Last spring I took six small businesses through AI implementation, and I'm trying to work out what's still unsolved for owners. Two questions about [company]:

Since you said that, what has actually changed – the work itself, or who does it?

And who on your team picked it up first – and was it who you expected?

Happy to send back what other founders tell me.

-Jefferson

## 4. A guest spot or a topic for their audience (doors: hosts, editors, organizers)

**Subject:** Question about [show, publication or room]

[First name] –

[One thing from their show, room or page: an episode, a guest, a line, quoted exactly.]

Last spring I took six small businesses through AI implementation, and I'm trying to work out what's still unsolved for owners once the AI session has happened. So here's a topic for [show], if it's useful: the team has had the training, a few people use AI every day, and everyone else has gone back to the old way. What does the owner do now?

It fits because [one line on why their audience is the one to answer it].

-Jefferson

For an organizer rather than a host (a speaker-relations manager, a chapter director), the give is two questions about how their room picks its topics, then "Glad to send back what I hear from other rooms."
