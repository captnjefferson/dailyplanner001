## 2. The person read

**Who he is.** John-Scott Dixon sold things on the web before most companies had a site, and he leads with it. Insight's president told him to put the company online in late 1994. He says he led the team that built "the third e-commerce site in the world" in 1995. Sprint PCS hired him to turn its site into a sales channel. In 2004 he left corporate life "to pursue a lifelong ambition as an entrepreneur." He holds a Missouri MBA and a law license ("Also an attorney, but don't hold it against me!").

He names his misses lightly:
- eShop Plaza, a 1995 dial-up mall, did not get "more than $10,000 in sales before it died or we quit."
- Semanticator, the personalization software he pitched to TechCrunch in 2008, leaves no trace after 2009.
- His 2024 AI-skills community stopped growing at about 150 members.

What he is proud of now is building with his own hands again: "Production code shipped that morning," from an EO Forum retreat in Panama.

**What he cares about.** Five themes recur across twenty years:
- **Measurement:** "If we can't test it, we don't do it!" (2010).
- **Ownership and no lock-in:** "You own every line of code… No lock-in" (2026).
- **Authenticity and excellence:** "Is your company authentic?" (2006); "Only the best is 'good enough'" (2014); Philippians 4:8 as his business standard (2025).
- **Working from anywhere:** "Work Anywhere | Reduce Costs | Live Better" (2006); "Code shipped in transit" (2026).
- **Humans with machines:** "I'm all about embracing AI and becoming collaborative with it" (Dec 2024); "AI handles the how… But it doesn't know what to build" (2026).

He praises clients with a vision and chapter volunteers. He mocks "vibe-coded software," "move fast and break things," plugin-stacked WordPress sites, and the mid-90s Sprint websites he once fixed: "I know because I was there!"

**Motivations and current behavior** (each INFERENCE rests on two or more signals in the evidence list):
- **M1.** The 2026 tools brought him back to building, and he wants the 1995 builder seen again.
