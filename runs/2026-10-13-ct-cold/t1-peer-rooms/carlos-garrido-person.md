## 2. The person read

**Who he is.** Carlos Garrido grew up in Manchester, the son of a Greek mother and a Spanish father. He qualified as a chartered accountant and spent years doing deals, first in banking and then in-house at AstraZeneca. In 2009 AstraZeneca sent him to Miami to lead Latin American business development because they thought he spoke Spanish. He didn't, never corrected them, and still tells the story as a joke on himself [MCLW; HTS25]. His brother Antonio, a Sandler client, asked him to write a business plan for an investor visa. Carlos fell for the business, and in 2013 the two of them opened Sandler Training Miami [MCLW; 305-family]. He is open about the hard years:
- Carolina, now his wife and co-chair, "stuck by me … even when it looked doomed" [SOM].
- Every business, he says, "is starving for two years" [MCLW].
- On LinkedIn (from the lead's read) he wrote about being evicted on Christmas Eve and being 100 lbs overweight [LIR].

Antonio retired to France around 2022, and Carlos bought him out [MCLW; 305-cofounders]. He is proud of the buyout, of his team, and of being a Vistage member, speaker and chair at once. He says all three roles "happened by chance" [E16 11:26].

**What he cares about.** He has repeated the same ideas for years:
- Selling means inspiring a great decision. Taking orders is not selling [E16 21:23; MCLW].
- You own the outcome. He calls a lost deal "on your watch, buddy" [E16 27:38].
- A business that depends on its founder is worth less. He states it as "your valuation scales only when your absence doesn't threaten the business" [305-sell] and, on LinkedIn, "Your sales system today is your valuation tomorrow" [LIR].
- Founders shouldn't go it alone. Entrepreneurship is "too hard to do it alone" [E16 52:36].
- Discipline and health: 75 Hard and half-marathon training [HTS25; LIR].

He mocks order-takers who are paid like sellers, people who blame their leads, and CEOs who want to hand AI off to an agency. He praises Bezos for the value he has created [E16].
