## 2. The person read

Ryan Flanagan grew up in Delaware and worked in sales. After the University of Delaware (2016) he sold for Stanley Black & Decker while "opening up LLC after LLC," looking for an e-commerce business that would let him "work from anywhere." In late 2018, Yağız Aksu, an acquaintance in Türkiye, wanted a U.S. partner for poultry equipment. Flanagan, who "had never raised a chicken," said yes. The first container, 22 pallets, filled his parents' house, and Sue and Neil Flanagan shipped orders and answered phones until they retired. He tells that story in nearly every interview. He is proud of what came out of it:
- "tens of millions in sales … mind blowing to say that out loud";
- four straight years on the Inc. 5000;
- a team he refused to "lay them off and move to a different state," "no matter the cost."

The losses he names:
- earlier ventures where "nothing proved sustainable";
- banks that saw a mid-twenties founder with a foreign partner as "a high risk," even after a first year of more than a million dollars in sales.

He comes back to five themes:
1. **Customers and teaching them.** "Our customers are everything" (Aug 2026). "Our equipment requires education" (Mar 2026).
2. **Roots and loyalty.** "It all comes back to my roots" (Oct 2024). "a tight circle of really good genuine people" (Aug 2026).
3. **Helping younger founders.** "This is what it's all about for me. Helping the next generation" (Jan 2022).
4. **Using what already exists, strategically.** "The infrastructure is there. It's about using it strategically" (Mar 2026). "What you put in is what you get out" (Jul 2025).
5. **Knowing what goes into food,** first for flocks and now for dogs. "ingredients you actually recognize" (Sep 2026).

He praises free help, Amazon's toolkit and good people. He knocks anything "crazy expensive" and AI that "sucks at analytics right now" (Aug 2026).

**INFERENCE: he and Aksu build a portfolio of companies, and the newer ones start from a price he found too high as a buyer.**
- Exhibit Creators (2023) began because the partners were "frustrated with inflated prices" for trade-show booths.
- Oldfield Pets began because treats for his rescue dog were "crazy expensive."
- His Inc. column says he and Aksu "have built multiple companies together."

What he wants next is reach: a UK store, Chewy, Tractor Supply and Petco for Oldfield, and a showroom model "to replicate across North America."

**INFERENCE: where he won't use AI is the side of the business his partner runs.**
- His June 2026 Inc. column gives him sales, marketing, advertising and technology. Aksu has finance, operations and inventory.
- His team went "all in on #AI" in 2025.
- Yet he "will not have it do projections and forecasts."
- He calls inventory planning "one of the most critical parts of the operation." In a seasonal business, a wrong forecast ties up cash in inventory.

**INFERENCE: he is building an advisor identity alongside the operator one.**
- The 2025 Who's Who profile says his "current focus includes mentoring emerging entrepreneurs."
- His LinkedIn headline adds "Mentor," and the profile has a booking link.
- Amazon and the SBDC both say he mentors students and young founders.

**How he talks.** Short, plain and upbeat:
- idioms ("wasn't up to snuff," "checks all our boxes");
- cheerful overstatement ("I guarantee it!");
- a video-game image ("It's a power-up 🍄");
- capitals for emphasis ("DEFINITELY");
- blunt one-liners when he disagrees.

His stories have family in them and end on a number. He uses no jargon and cites no books.

**Where he shows up:** Inc. (Inc. 5000 2023–26; his column from June 2026; the Founder Group Chat, Aug 2026); Amazon (seller story, Mar 2026; "See you on the Hill" advocacy); the Delaware SBDC and UD classrooms; trade shows (IPPE; Global Pet Expo and SuperZoo, 2025–26). No podcasts found.

**What he consumes:** not found beyond what he names: Amazon's seller tools, the SBDC, Inc.'s founder community, and peers. On AI he says to "be a sponge" and listen to how others use the tools (Nov 2025).
