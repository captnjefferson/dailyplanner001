## 2. The person read

Paul Downs finished a Penn engineering degree in 1985, worked as a carpenter's helper in West Philadelphia, and started making furniture in 1986 with "$5,000 I had inherited from my grandmother" and a rented rowhouse shell in Manayunk. He took a 50/50 partner in 2002, when he was "40 and kind of stuck"; in 2003 Google made his conference-table page a top result, and the shop became a conference-table maker. In 2008 the partnership broke over money ("one night he took our cash reserves and paid down our line of credit"); he laid off half his people, paid himself $36,000 and came "within a day of running out of money" in January 2010. Sure he was about to fail, he wrote to Loren Feldman at the New York Times because its small-business blog had "nobody who's about to fail." Feldman hired him: 174 posts over four and a half years, then *Boss Life* (2015): "I am a survivor, but not a financial success." He and the partner later reconciled.

His family is on his own record. He met his wife, Nancy Bea Miller, at Penn. They have three sons, Hugh and the twins Peter and Henry, and used WIC vouchers for the twins in the early 1990s. Henry is "a severely autistic young man incapable of speech" (Penn Gazette, 2015); in January 2026 Paul said "my disabled son has a timespan of about 15 seconds." A son is marrying in 2026. "My primary loyalty is to, first, my family; second, to my employees. They're my tribe" (2026-03-03). He calls himself "pretty liberal" but takes no public stands, because half his clients disagree and his employees would pay.

**What he returns to:**
1. **A custom shop can't be reduced to a model:** pricing advice assumes "statistically relevant conclusions can be drawn… In a small company like mine, I have found the opposite to be true" (2014); "An image of a thing that looks cool is not a design" (2026-06-02).
2. **Cash:** "I'm obsessed with cash flow" (2026-09-29), on a spreadsheet he has kept since 2008.
3. **The tribe:** he insures employees' children "because I believe having children is a good thing" and laid people off so "I would have the option to bring them back" (2025).
4. **Candor over theory:** one useful sentence per business book is "doing pretty well" (2026-06-16); "You only hear from the winners" (2025-12-23).
5. **Being found:** Google "put me on the path to prosperity, and I want to make sure that we don't miss the next boat" (2025-11-18).

He mocks cold-email drips ("the 55th person who offers to clean my bathrooms") and "AI slop"; he praises peers who give as much as they get.

**INFERENCE: his "no" on AI covers the shop floor and design, not his own desk.** He uses ChatGPT for GSA checklists, a trademark renewal, rent math, the R&D credit and his FAQ, and asked on air for an agent that learns his admin workflows.

**INFERENCE: the open question underneath is the handoff of what's in his head.** He is "queuing the company up to be sold," two long-time employees are interested, and he keeps naming the hard part as teaching "what I actually do all day."

**INFERENCE: he moves when a peer hands him a specific, cheap fix.** The careers page went up 2025-12-12, the month he told Jaci Russo "we have never put anything about hiring on our website"; he invited Ted Wolf's team into the shop; "It's good advice. Sure, I'll take it" (to Jay Goltz).

**How he talks:** long, reasoned turns built on numbers; dry jokes ("How would you like your payment to be sent?"); vivid images ("7,000 balloons shoving in my face"; "pee in my Cheerios"); casual profanity; pointed questions back ("How's it working for the group?"); turns ending "Does that make sense?" His columns closed on "What would you do if you were me?"

**Where he shows up:** 21 Hats (15 episodes in 12 months), formerly the NYT and Vistage; a 2018 trade keynote. No personal social accounts found.

**What he consumes:** *Traction*; ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini; Scribe; QuickBooks under his own spreadsheets. Nothing else found.
