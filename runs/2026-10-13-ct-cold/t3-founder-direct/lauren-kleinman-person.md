## 2. Who she is

Lauren Kleinman was "raised and inspired by a family of entrepreneurs," running charity lemonade stands as a child and importing Razor scooters with an uncle at eight. She studied at USC, then joined StartEngine, Scopely and Ritual before starting Dreamday in 2019 as "a one-woman (yours truly!) consultancy." She is married to Andy, whose work is with Netflix and FIFA, and is "a mom of two," a daughter, Leia, and a son, Alexander (5 and 7 in March 2025), plus "two golden retrievers."

She is open about health. "A couple years ago, I hit a period of burnout and insomnia… One day in a forum session… I just broke down and began crying." Eight women in a YPO spousal forum got her through it (YPO, the Young Presidents' Organization, is a peer network for company heads under 45; members meet monthly in confidential groups of eight called forums). She now belongs to YPO Santa Monica and became her forum's moderator this September. She is a certified yoga instructor and reads "about spirituality and self-growth."

Community is a stated motive: she is co-chair, with "my dear friend Dr. Deepika Chopra," of the Women's Philanthropy Circle at LA's Cayton Children's Museum, and judges the Boys & Girls Club's Youth of the Year. On AI she believes "you have to understand something yourself before you can lead others through it." She calls herself a Virgo who finds "so much pleasure in following protocol."

> "I'd spent months teaching myself Claude Code… I built things I was [somewhat] proud of, but at one point, the late nights stopped feeling like progress and started feeling like a wall. I am the founder and CEO of two agencies, but I was also trying to be an AI engineer, and that just wasn't working for my mental health or bloodshot eyes. So we made a hire." https://laurenakleinman.substack.com/p/were-not-a-tech-company-we-hired

Status: resolved by a consultant phase, then a full-time AI engineer. **How she describes the hire:** "We moved because we'd validated enough use cases." "Ours told us early on that what he's able to do for us today would have taken 15 engineers and a year-long process just a couple of years ago." Close: "We're only a few weeks in, and I already have my answer." The engineer is not named in her posts; name not found.

**2. Getting the whole team to use AI, not just the engineer.** Same post:
> "Here's the thing nobody really talks about when they talk about hiring an AI engineer: the hire is just the start. The real work is making AI feel natural for everyone around them… We're working to close that gap."

Status: open and in progress: AI written into SOPs in Trainual, manual mastery required before a tool is given, a hackathon after each training cycle, "Innovator of the Month," an internal AI task force, and "AI proficiency now factors into promotions and tenure."
