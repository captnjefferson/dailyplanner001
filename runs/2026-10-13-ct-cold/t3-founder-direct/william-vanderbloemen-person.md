## 2. The person read

William Vanderbloemen is a former Presbyterian pastor who sizes himself up bluntly: "I'm a salesperson. I love to persuade."

**Path.** He went to Princeton Seminary meaning to become a professor. Instead he pastored churches in North Carolina, Alabama and Houston, becoming senior pastor of First Presbyterian Houston at 31. After that came corporate HR and a job at another search firm. He dates his own firm, built with his wife Adrienne, to 2008. He started it, he jokes, with "a religion and philosophy degree" that was "not super helpful."

**Pride.** He credits timing over brains: "I'm not smart. We just got lucky." He is proud of three things:
- the "top 30,000" candidates behind *Be the Unicorn*;
- HubSpot using his firm on its IPO roadshow;
- making search "normal" for churches.

**Losses.** He names his losses as easily:
- His first four key hires were "all like me."
- The window to sell "has already shut."
- The pandemic came "pretty damn close" to a depression for his firm.
- He once flagged an unstable boss without "enough transparency," then left. He says: "I don't think I did enough."
- His grandfather warned him that a pastor who makes "the wrong group of people mad" is "done." His verdict: "he was right."

*Family-health context, for tone and timing only.* He has said publicly that Adrienne, his co-founder, has cancer. In September 2026 he reported "no evidence of active cancer." He called the experience "a bit of a wake-up call" about how he spends his time.

**Five themes he keeps coming back to:**
1. **AI as a pendulum.** He swings between "This is going to be amazing" and "We are cooked." He settles it with Leviticus 10:10, sorting what is "permanent" from "a passing fad."
2. **Discernment as the product.** "We get paid for human discernment."
3. **H2H.** His shorthand for human-to-human contact.
4. **Being replaceable.** "I cannot be the lid to this thing."
5. **A fickle market.** "Candidates are more fickle than ever."

**What he praises:** responsiveness, curiosity, hustle, and hires from service jobs who never finished college.

**What he mocks:** AI "slop," SEO vendors who feel like "local protection," airports that look like a "pajama convention," and anyone who calls a 100-person church "small."

**INFERENCE: he is building his own exit from the daily seat.** Signals:
- In January he said the firm "will probably need younger leadership" within five years.
- In September he said "Hiring a CEO? Very possible."
- A younger leader in training now sits in his office.
- Four "Director of Consulting" titles appeared on the team page after July 30.

**INFERENCE: he is handing AI from himself to the firm.** Signals:
- His learning time fell from "an hour every day" in February to "three days a week, 90 minutes" in September.
- A September 25 job posting puts one manager over "the company tech stack" and asks for "basic programming."
- He called 2026 the year of a "more cohesive set of tools."

Taken together, he wants a firm that outlasts him and a team that spends its hours on people.

**How he talks.** He talks like a preacher and says so: "it's gonna be long and three points, and it all starts with the same letter." One answer can hold scripture, Roman roads, golf and a Terminator joke, and he flags his own rabbit trails as he goes. In writing he is shorter and confessional: "William here. I need to make a confession: I was wrong." When he doesn't know, one word does it: "Nope."

**Where he shows up:**
- Loren Feldman's *21 Hats* panel, four times in 2026
- his own *Vanderbloemen Leadership Podcast*
- Praxis
- AI cohorts in Austin
- a yearly gathering of executive pastors from large churches

**What he reads and uses:**
- On AI: *The AI-Driven Leader* and *AI Snake Oil*.
- For managing younger staff, he recommends *Generations* and *The Anxious Generation*.
- He quotes Dave Ramsey, Craig Groeschel and Gary Vaynerchuk.
- He scans Google News after his morning routine and reads a chronological Bible with Adrienne.
- He pays for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok.
