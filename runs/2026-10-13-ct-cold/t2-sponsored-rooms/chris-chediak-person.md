## 2. The person read

Chris Chediak is a Sacramento deal lawyer of the old regional school: one firm for decades, one city, a client list built on the Central Valley's middle market. He took a B.A. and a B.S. at UC Davis in 1980, his J.D. at USC in 1983, and was admitted that December. By 2007 the firm on his door was Weintraub Genshlea Chediak. What he built is a corporate practice the firm called "Sacramento's largest" in 2021, "Led by Chris Chediak, Jim Clarke, and Bradus": outside counsel to NutraCea, later RiceBran Technologies, from 2005 to at least 2020; mezzanine deals in 2008 for the Central Valley Fund, now CVF Capital Partners, which co-hosts Business Focus; and a long side career in the wiring of California entity law, with CEB chapters, a 2007 award for more than ten years of service to CEB, and a 2020 term chairing the state bar section's LLC committee, where he led a proposed fix to the LLC cancellation statute. What he is proud of, in his words, is the client outcome: "a positive outcome for our client after many months of negotiations between the two parties as well as with the Brazilian government" (March 2008). No failure story is on the record. One personal fact is on the record, and it explains his biggest current commitment: on the Shriners announcement he wrote that his daughter "was a patient for many years" (seed: LinkedIn). Six years on that board and the 2027–2028 chair follow from it.

He returns to four things. California is a hard place to close: "Definitely, that has killed deals before … 'No, California regulations — the law — do not allow that.' And sometimes they're surprised" (Oct 2025). Selling is normal here: there has "always been a lot of companies in the Sacramento area that get acquired" (Oct 2025). Capital for the Central Valley: "their investment and mission of continued growth in California's Central Valley" (Mar 2008). And records discipline as technology changes: "Train all personnel in the appropriate use of email. Email takes 20 seconds to prepare, but it stays around for a long time" (May 2011). He praises skill and dedication in colleagues. He mocks nothing on the record.

INFERENCE: his public energy is moving from running the practice to governance and civic leadership. The Corporate chair passed in February 2026 from Clarke to Chris Anderson and Justin Borrowdale; his 2025 bio moved Shriners to the top and marked older boards "former"; in September he was elected Shriners board chair for 2027–2028. INFERENCE: Business Focus is a client-relationship room he anchors, not a marketing event he runs. CVF was his client in 2008; he is on every edition since 2020 that names a firm speaker; a 2020 panelist and the 2025 keynote came from CVF's own circle. INFERENCE: his interest in AI is a practical buyer's interest. He has said nothing on AI in his own voice; the firm's AI writing comes from IP and employment, not Corporate; he follows Harvey and subscribes to a local AI-automation newsletter (seed: LinkedIn). What that suggests he wants next: a sober, value-first AI conversation he can bring to the owners and boards he advises.

He talks courteously, client-first, low-key. In print he writes like a bar bulletin: "Covered entities would be well-advised to continue preparing." Out loud he explains by acting out the dialogue (the buyer wants X, "we say to them, 'No'") and hedges ("so to speak," "kind of"). Online he leaves short, warm notes with double exclamation points and a trailing dash (seed: LinkedIn).

He shows up at Business Focus (2020–2026), a CLA Annual Meeting panel (Monterey, October 2019), the CAHL "Learning From Leaders" program beside Shriners' administrator (November 29, 2023), and in Comstock's (2011, 2025).
