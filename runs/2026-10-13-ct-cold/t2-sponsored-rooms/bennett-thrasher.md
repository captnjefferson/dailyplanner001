## 3. How speakers are chosen and paid

- **Who chooses:** not stated on any page.
  - The one booking on record ran through marketing. All American Entertainment (AAE), a speakers bureau, says its client contact was Allie Donahue, then Marketing Manager ("This was an annual event, and we really wanted to up our game!").
  - AAE "provided the client with a qualified list of vetted virtual speaker options within their specified budget." The firm "strongly preferred to have a speaker with name recognition."
- **Who owns it now:** the printed contact (Fouts) and the reel host (Lawrence) are in marketing, and Hoover closes the day. The person read's inference is that Hoover is the senior owner.
- **Paid:** fees are not stated.
  - **Grade A.** Washington Speakers Bureau lists Mohamed Massaquoi at "$40K - $70K" (https://www.wsb.com/speakers/mohamed-massaquoi/) and Pascal Finette at "$25K - $40K" (https://www.wsb.com/speakers/pascal-finette/).
  - **INFERENCE (two signals): the firm pays the fees itself.** (1) Tickets are free every year on record. (2) No sponsor is named on any 2023 to 2026 page.
- **Pattern:** each year on record (2024 to 2026) has an economist, a motivational or "mindset" keynote, one AI session (Hays 2024, Carr 2025, Huels 2026), and two sessions led by the firm's own people. Tutterow is the only outside speaker on record booked twice.
