Hot off the press! New in Therapeutic Innovation & Regulatory Science, open access: "Real-World Evidence for Drug Approvals: Moving Toward Greater Regulatory Clarity." A decade into the 21st Century Cures Act, real-world evidence still rarely makes it into drug approvals. The usual explanation is data quality. Data quality is critical. But, we argue the more stubborn constraint is operational: even fit-for-use RWD gets stuck on three unresolved policy questions.
1. Data standards. FDA's submission requirements are built around CDISC, designed for protocol-driven trials. Forcing heterogeneous RWD into SDTM and ADaM is costly and can obscure provenance. FHIR helps at the data-exchange layer but is not a regulatory-grade submission format on its own.
2. Data access and inspection. It is still unclear when sponsors must submit patient-level versus aggregated data, and how FDA inspects source records. The December 2025 device guidance signals a shift, but its implications for drugs and biologics are undefined. Federated and hybrid architectures, already proven in Sentinel and DARWIN EU, deserve a serious look.
3. Human subject protections. Applying 21 CFR Parts 50 and 56 to non-interventional studies creates real ambiguity. We propose distinguishing three categories of non-interventional designs by risk profile, and clarifying where consent and IRB review actually apply.
For each barrier we offer concrete steps FDA can take now. Thank you to the amazing co-authors Rasika Kalamegham (Genentech/Roche), Nicole Mahoney (Novartis), Rose Purcell (Takeda), and Mike D'Ambrosio (Parexel). Read it here. Comments and pushback welcome.
