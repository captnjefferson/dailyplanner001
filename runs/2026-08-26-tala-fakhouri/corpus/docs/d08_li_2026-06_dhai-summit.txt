Thank you Dr. Amir Lahav for the invitation to the Digital Health & AI Innovation Summit (DHAI), and for curating a summit that transcends the "usual" AI conversations. I had the pleasure of joining Jeremy Walsh for "Can AI Regulate AI? Staying Competitive in a World of AI-Driven Drugs and Diagnostics." A few points to summarize our conversation: Jeremy noted that the bottleneck is no longer the science, it's the review process. Discovery is accelerating, but the ecosystem can only move as fast as regulatory decision-making. I agree, with one caveat: clinical research can only be compressed so far. When we are running experiments on human biology, biology sets the pace of pivotal trials. What we can improve is everything around it: seamless exchange of meaningful data with regulators and a system that delivers decisions with greater speed, predictability, and consistency. For early-phase trials, the challenge is not generating more data. It is understanding what regulators need at each decision point and creating mechanisms to provide those signals continuously rather than through the traditional collect, pause, submit, wait cycle. Angela Holmes, MSBME expanded on this concept in her presentation on Real-Time Continuous Trials. Her vision of a trial that "speaks for itself" captures where the field is heading, but she also highlighted why this is much harder than simply building better dashboards. The real challenges are statistical and structural. The technology is largely here. The next challenge is aligning policy, incentives, and infrastructure to make it work at scale.
