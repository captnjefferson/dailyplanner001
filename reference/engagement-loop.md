# The engagement loop — how a play runs week to week

The research half of the play (org cards, people maps, corpus match) was never the problem — the gap was a repeatable weekly execution rhythm. Comments never went up because each one was a bespoke decision. This closes that: a standing loop where the prep is done for him and his part takes minutes.

Built 2026-08-25 from the Layla Young call (Attio d2efdc57) + Jefferson's framing on that call: *"I want all of those people to, once a week, maybe twice every two days-ish, see me popping up somewhere in the media that they're consuming... raising the awareness floor... then how do we elevate that in drips and drabs over what feels like a natural time period."* Same tactics apply personally, professionally, to Adaptiverse, and to Lisa — only the positioning changes (his ruling on the call).

## Layla's observations this is built on (2026-08-25)

1. **Target the person, not the business.** Org accounts can barely engage on LinkedIn (three-steps-deep backend, can only interact with followed accounts) — "no wonder LinkedIn posts flop." People check their personal feeds. Every touch comes from HIS profile (or Lisa's), never the org page.
2. **"Engagement time" is the underused lever** — regular commenting/liking on other people's content. She flags a big drop-off when it stops. It's a scheduled activity, not a mood.
3. **Casual, conversational interaction builds the momentum that carries the important posts** — the ones that get you "the funding, job, partnership" (his words back to her, she confirmed).
4. **Ask questions.** Posts and comments that invite a response fill their comment sections even at mediocre quality. Never bare-repost — add a take or a question so a human starts a conversation.
5. **Scroll-stoppers work** (her chess-puzzle example: "solve this in 3 moves, message me" → real meetups). Occasional visual/puzzle posts (e.g. the 3D-printed jet engine build) earn conversational replies that pure insight posts don't.
6. **Long-form insight still matters but hits less often** — it's the portfolio/credibility layer someone finds AFTER a touch lands, not the touch itself. Post it; expect low engagement; that's fine.

These sit on top of his own data: his highest-reach post ever was a reply into someone else's live thread (3,286 impressions, ~4x his best original). The loop is mostly OTHER people's threads on purpose.

## The loop (per active play)

**Inputs (from the org card, already built):** 2–3 target people · their feed URLs · corpus match (which of his pieces backs which theme) · timing/kill rules.

**Twice a week, ~20 minutes each ("engagement blocks", scheduled like any block):**

1. **Fixer preps the queue before each block** — sweep the targets' feeds (logged-in Chrome, read-only), pick 2–4 live touches, and for each hand him: the post link, the suggested touch type, the ANGLE, and which of his pieces backs it. Never the prose — comments are his ([[jefferson-voice-and-register]]).
2. **He executes in the block.** Touch menu, in descending order of value:
   - Reply-comment in a target's live thread (the proven best move — especially where the author replies to strangers)
   - A comment that ASKS something (invites the author to respond)
   - Likes/supports on target-adjacent content (cheap floor-raiser between comments)
   - His own question-post or repost-with-a-take (momentum layer; can use Adaptiverse content as the conversation starter)
   - Occasional scroll-stopper (build photos, puzzles)
3. **Cadence guardrails (ratified 8/20):** each target sees him ~1–2×/week; spread across 2–3 people per org; never 4 comments on one person in a week.
4. **Fixer logs every touch** in `state/measurement.json` (comment URL, target, date) and captures engagement + profile views on the standing cadence (see `measurement-instrument.md`). Kill reviews read the log.

**The sequence rules stay untouched:** awareness precedes the application, always. Submit per the timing model, then message 2–3 targets that the app is in.

## Division of labor (unchanged)
Comments/messages/submits: HE writes and sends. Fixer: queue prep, angles + backing pieces, logging, measurement, kill-review data.
